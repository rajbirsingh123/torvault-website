No safe is truly fireproof — the correct term is fire-resistant or fire-rated. A UL 72 rating like “1–Hr / 350°F” means the interior stayed below 350°F for one hour inside a furnace that can reach 1,700°F outside the safe — not that the contents are immune to fire indefinitely. Match the duration rating to your realistic fire-department response time.

Ratings & Certification

UL (Underwriters Laboratories) independently tests safes against real tools — prybars, drills, saws, cutting torches — for a set net working time (the clock only runs while a tool is actively working the safe). A TL‑15 safe resists a professional attack with common tools for 15 minutes; TL‑30 for 30, on every side. It’s the same standard insurers use to set coverage limits. Full breakdown on our Certified Standard page.

RSC (Residential Security Container) is tested for 5 minutes net working time against common hand tools, on all sides. TL‑15 triples the working time to 15 minutes, adds power drills and pressure-applying devices to the tool kit, but is only tested against the door. RSC is Torvault’s Fire & Burglary line; TL‑15 is the next tier up.

No — burglary ratings (RSC, TL‑15, TL‑30) and fire ratings (UL 72 Class 350/150/125) are tested completely independently. A high burglary rating says nothing about fire performance and vice versa. If you need both, confirm the safe carries a documented rating for each, not just one.
