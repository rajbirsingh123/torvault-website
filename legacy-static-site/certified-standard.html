Certified Standard

Every rating, explained.

UL ratings aren’t a marketing tier — they’re the result of an independent lab physically attacking a safe with real tools and a stopwatch. Here’s exactly what each classification on a Torvault spec plate means.

How UL Testing Works

Net working time, not wall-clock time.

Underwriters Laboratories (UL) certifies safes by having trained technicians — effectively professional safecrackers — attack a production unit with a defined kit of tools. The result is measured in net working time: the clock only runs while a tool is actively working the safe, and stops the instant it’s removed. A safe doesn’t pass by surviving an hour in a workshop — it passes by resisting a fixed amount of continuous, focused attack.

Entry Level

UL RSC

Residential Security Container — the baseline UL burglary classification, and the rating behind Torvault’s Fire & Burglary line.

  • 5 minutes net working time
  • Tested on all sides, not just the door
  • Common mechanical hand tools only
  • Combination or key lock, UL-listed
Mid Tier

UL TL‑15

Tool-resistant, 15-minute rating — the standard behind most Canadian bank branches and pharmacy safes.

  • 15 minutes net working time, door only
  • Hand tools, drills, punches, hammers
  • Pressure-applying devices permitted
  • Up to 200 lbs / freestanding not required
High Security

UL TL‑30

The rating behind Torvault’s flagship line — same tool kit as TL‑15, plus power tools, on all six sides.

  • 30 minutes net working time
  • Tested on the body, not just the door
  • Adds abrasive cutting wheels & power saws
  • High-speed carbide drills, cutting torches
UL 72 Fire Endurance

What survives a 1,700°F furnace.

UL 72 is the standard fire-resistance test for record and data protection equipment. A locked safe is placed inside an industrial furnace that follows a controlled time-temperature curve reaching up to 1,700°F, while sensors continuously log the internal temperature. The safe fails the instant the interior exceeds its class threshold — anywhere inside the chamber.

Class 350

Interior stays below 350°F — the temperature at which paper begins to char. The standard for document and cash protection.

Class 150

Interior stays below 150°F — required for microfilm and other heat-sensitive media.

Class 125

Interior stays below 125°F — the tightest tolerance, for digital media, hard drives, and backup tape.

Duration (“–Hr”)

Paired with the class, e.g. 1–Hr / 350°F — how long the safe holds its class rating inside the furnace.

Why it matters for insurance: Most commercial property and cash-in-transit policies in Canada set coverage limits directly against a safe’s UL rating — a policy might cap cash coverage at $2,500 in an unrated container versus $50,000+ behind a UL TL‑30 body. Before specifying a safe for a client, confirm the rating their insurer actually requires, not just what fits the budget.
Beyond TL‑30

UL’s burglary scale continues past TL‑30 for specialized applications — TRTL ratings add torch resistance, and TRTL/TXTL variants extend net working time to 60 minutes for vault-class applications. Torvault’s current three collections cover the range Canadian dealers request most; higher-tier vault doors are available on request through our dealer program.
